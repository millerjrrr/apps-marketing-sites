## Background

I was always academically strong. I graduated from Trinity College Dublin with a degree in Mathematics, finishing near the top of my class.

On paper, that background should translate well into poker. Logic. Probability. Pattern recognition.

But something important was missing.

Mathematics trained me to solve problems alone. It rewarded “figure it out yourself.” That mindset made me independent, but it did not give me a research culture mindset. I did not learn how to collaborate, to research or systematically refine heuristics. That gap slowed my development in poker far more than I realized at the time.

---

## Early Poker Years

I first played online around 2012 to 2013. It was curiosity, not commitment. I didn’t know anyone in poker and had no structured learning environment.

In late 2016, I took a part-time job as a poker dealer in Dublin. That changed everything.

Even with very limited poker knowledge, I could see how soft the live games were. I started playing 1/2 live with €50 buy-ins, essentially push-or-folding at first. As my bankroll grew, I moved to €100 buy-ins, always using conservative bankroll management. Poker was still a weekend pursuit.

At the same time:

- I graduated from TCD
- I joined ALDI Ireland head office as a statistics/strategy analyst

I performed well in corporate life. But poker was quietly outperforming expectations.

In 2018, playing only weekends and holidays, I made €38k from poker over roughly 1000 hours.

In February 2019, I left my corporate job to play full time.

---

## First Full-Time Phase (Live Focus)

I traveled to the US and played 2/5 in Los Angeles and later at Borgata Hotel Casino & Spa.

At this stage I was:

- Smart
- Disciplined
- Underskilled in theory

My theoretical background consisted mainly of Upswing content and reading _No Limit Hold’em for Advanced Players_ by Matthew Janda.

I was winning, but I did not understand the game deeply.

Then the pandemic hit.

---

## Online Poker Era (2020–2023)

With live poker shut down, I committed to online.

I:

- Bought MMASherdog's High Stakes Course
- Using Simple Postflop I studied solver outputs seriously for the first time
- Memorized preflop ranges (from MMAs preflop Bible)
- Started at 2NL and built an "online role" progressing to 200NL by the end of March 2021

From 2021 to 2023, I:

- Played mainly 200NL online
- Made multiple trips to Houston for live games
- Made over $100k each year

I studied heavily with GTO Wizard and built "near-GTO" ranges that I memorized and are the ranges I use with Griddier today.

On paper, this was a success phase.

But internally, I felt stuck.

I could not break into 500NL.  
The ecosystem was tightening.  
Rake structures were worsening.  
Edges were shrinking.

My approach was very solver-heavy and memorization-based and really: just bad.

---

## Brazil and the Software Detour

With children planned and live poker less viable as a result, I started looking at other options. This was also motivated by my
decision to get really, really good at Portuguese. I had decided I was going to live in Brazil for the foreseeable future and
if I was going to live there I would need to be able to speak the language. Duolingo was not going to cut it.

In September 2023, I launched into building a language learning app for advanced learners. That evolved into an intense 100+ hour per week obsession.

I built:

- A full stack app
- Infrastructure
- Business systems
- Branding
- Marketing

I learned more in 2024 than in any previous year of my life.

Not just about software.  
About business.  
About psychology.  
About discipline.  
About obsession.

The app was well built but did not achieve the validation needed to become a viable business. The market was brutal.

In April 2025, I presented at Web Summit Rio but with no support and few positive interactions... well "you gotta know when to fold up".

The project is now paused as a primary business venture, though it remains a valuable long-term asset and potentially a portfolio piece.

---

## Returning to Poker

Watching the Cash Game World Championship on CoinPoker reignited something in me.

It wasn’t the money. It was the thinking.

Listening to elite players verbalize their decision processes shifted something internally. I began thinking less about memorizing solver outputs and more about:

- Strategic reasoning
- Range interaction
- Incentive structures
- Exploit windows
- Population tendencies

In 2025 I returned to online poker seriously.

The games were much tougher than in 2023. Rake was worse (Thanks GG) and Party was effectively dead.

I transitioned to PokerStars, playing 100NL and 200NL, mostly Zoom with some regular tables when games were slow.

It took me a while to shake off the rust but right now I feel like I am playing better than ever. I've been doing less work with the solver and dedicating more time to really thinking about my game and trying to identify and tackle both technical and mental game leaks.

I've also started working with a coach, Luke Johnson @KnowYourPoker.
